## WHOOP 4.0 strap
- Protocol reference: noop's `docs/PROTOCOL_WHOOP4.md` and `docs/PROTOCOL_SENSORS.md` (repo `ryanbr/noop`).
- The phone's Bluetooth must be off: the strap serves one central at a time.
- Bond the strap the way it asks, not with `bluetoothctl pair`. A manual pair leaves command writes unanswered, and BlueZ then rejects every later write with `InProgress`. Instead: `bluetoothctl remove <mac>`, then in `bluetoothctl` run `agent NoInputNoOutput`, `default-agent`, `connect <mac>`, subscribe to `61080003`/`4`/`5` with `gatt.notify on`, and answer `yes` to the strap's "Accept pairing" request.
- `just dev calibrate` serves the calibrator on http://127.0.0.1:7878 and appends labelled takes to `$XDG_DATA_HOME/noop-gestures/takes.jsonl`; it only answers loopback Host headers and same-origin writes.
- `just dev probe [seconds] [--imu-stream] [--queries]` runs the live IMU probe: command 63 enables type-43 frames, 1928-byte ones carry 100 accel + 100 gyro samples, delivered about once per second. It prints the ATT MTU, each batch's fragment count and first-to-last fragment time, and every other frame as hex. `--queries` also sends the read-only gets 107, 130 and 65 and walks the feature-flag names with 117/118.
- A command reply is `[36, seq, command, request seq, result]` then the body; result 1 is success and 2 is pending.
- Lying unworn on 2026-11-26, the strap answered command 63 with pending and sent no type-43 frames in three probe runs. Stream timing and the double-tap event (type 48, event 14, printed by the probe as `event=14`) are unmeasured until a probe runs with it worn.
- BlueZ negotiates an ATT MTU of 247, so a batch is 8 notifications; the 1928-byte IMU frame arrives immediately before the 1932-byte optical frame. Live fragment probes while worn show frames landing on a 120 ms BLE cadence (8 × 120 ms = 960 ms per batch), with ~35–40% of IMU frames spilling across 2–4 connection events (+120 to +363 ms spread). btleplug cannot change the connection interval or PHY on Linux, and BlueZ only takes them from root: `btmgmt set-sysconfig` types 0017/0018 or `[LE]` `MinConnectionInterval`/`MaxConnectionInterval` in `main.conf`, and `btmgmt phy`. `btmgmt read-sysconfig` works unprivileged and shows the host asking for 30 to 50 ms.
- No get offers a smaller batch or a faster stream on this firmware: 107 (IMU stream) and 130 (R12 realtime stream) both read 0, 65 (packet config) reads `02 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 01 01 01 00 00 00`, and the only feature flag is `general_ab_test`.
- The u32 at frame offset 15 of type-43 frames is not Unix time on firmware seen so far; use arrival time.

## Recognizer and daemon
- Only one process may hold the strap: stop the calibrator before `just dev run`, or the daemon connects but receives no IMU data.
- `just dev evaluate` holds out one take at a time and prints the confusion matrix; run it after recording takes, before trusting `run`.
- The recognizer is 1-nearest-neighbour over the 25 samples before an activity peak and the 22 after it (gyro/200 dps and raw accel). Activity is gyro magnitude plus 50 dps per g that the acceleration magnitude strays from 1 g, so a knock with no rotation still registers. A peak is the maximum of those same 25 and 22 samples; idle takes become negative templates at every peak above 60. Re-sweep that weight with `evaluate` after recording knock takes.
- The detector has three tiers: (1) full-window classification once 22 samples follow a peak (requiring the nearest gesture to beat the nearest idle template by `FULL_IDLE_RATIO`² = 0.95²); (2) early peak classification for peaks 1 to 21 samples before the batch end using the mean of the 3 nearest templates per label (`EARLY_NEIGHBORS = 3`, `EARLY_DISTANCE_RATIO = 0.94`); (3) rising-edge classification at the final sample of a batch (`RISING_WINDOW = 16`, up to `RISING_MAX_LEAD = 25` samples before a gesture's peak, comparing against distinct gesture takes' best leads and active rising idle tails with `RISING_DISTANCE_RATIO = 0.90`). A confident rising-edge fire sets the same 100-sample cooldown so the upcoming peak in the next batch does not double-fire. Constants live in `src/recognizer/mod.rs`.
- Tried and dropped: a distance-margin reject rule on full windows and DTW both lost accuracy offline. The symmetric 60-sample window with a 60-sample look-ahead had a mean latency of 1130 ms and 70 long-idle false fires on the benchmark. A window of 60 before and 14 after with early prefixes from 5 samples had 621 ms and 56.
- `just dev run` runs one action per gesture, listed in `action` in `src/daemon.rs`: twist scrolls the focused app down 5 notches with `ydotool`; reverse twist sends niri `ToggleOverview`; left and right send `FocusColumnLeft` and `FocusColumnRight`, up and down send `FocusWorkspaceUp` and `FocusWorkspaceDown`; knock runs `openhome ir edifier mute`. Niri actions go as JSON lines over one persistent connection to `$NIRI_SOCKET` (`src/niri.rs`); after a compositor restart it reconnects to the socket of the same Wayland display. Swing labels are named after their action, so the takes were relabelled when bindings moved (backups sit next to `takes.jsonl`). `ydotool` talks to the `ydotoold` user service, whose socket is `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/.ydotool_socket` rather than the client's `/tmp` default, so the daemon sets `YDOTOOL_SOCKET` when it is unset; without it `ydotool` exits 0 and does nothing. A 100-sample cooldown, about 1 s, stops a return swing from firing the opposite gesture. The strap sends one batch per second, so latency depends on where the peak falls in its batch: the benchmark gives a median of 0.6 s and a p95 of 1.05 s.
- The strap samples at about 104 Hz, not 100, so a batch spans about 960 ms. `Timeline` counts samples, fits the sample period to the batch arrivals and stamps along the earliest-arrival line: a late batch does not move its samples, and no sample is dropped. A stamp trails the true sample time by the fastest transport time, which is unmeasured. Arrivals are read from a monotonic clock anchored to Unix time at each connect.
- The detector is keyed on sample count and ignores timestamps except to report the peak.
- `just dev run` logs per fire whether it fired on the full window or early and on how many samples past the peak, how long after the peak its batch arrived, the peak's index in its batch, how many later batches the look-ahead waited for and the detector's time, then each action's time: spawn to exit for a process, dispatch to niri's reply for a niri action.
- `just dev benchmark` replays every take, held out, at 28 placements through `Timeline` and `Detector` as 100-sample batches. It prints recognised, wrong and missed counts, latency from the peak to the firing batch (median, p95 and mean), how far the logged latency strays from it, and false fires over the long idle takes, per label and for all gestures together. Its 9.6 ms sample period and 60 to 120 ms transport delay are assumptions. On 2026-11-26 it gave 3137 recognised, 0 wrong and 279 missed of 3416 gesture replays, a median of 604 ms, a p95 of 1053 ms, a mean of 611 ms, 23 long-idle false fires, and 2 of the 40 short idle takes firing. Sweeping each constant on its own gave no clear gain: every alternative traded idle false fires against misses by about one take.
- 1932-byte type-43 frames carry the optical waveform: 100 signed 24-bit little-endian samples per frame from frame offset 42 with a stride of 4, so the same 100 Hz as the IMU. The bytes past sample 100 are not waveform. The channel's wavelength is unknown. Takes store it as an optional `optical` field, and the recognizer does not use it yet.
- Finger gestures were tried and dropped. A fist produces almost no wrist motion (peak activity mostly under 60), and its downward optical drift showed only about half the time. A thumb-to-index pinch, interleaved with idle takes, had an optical signal that matched idle; the first all-pinch run had separated only because of something in that session.
- `just dev record-idle --minutes 8` records one continuous idle take of normal work. `evaluate` replays every idle take of 60 s or more through the live detector, trained without that take, and prints how many gestures would have fired; that count is the false-trigger rate to drive down.
