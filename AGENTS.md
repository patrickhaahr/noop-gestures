## WHOOP 4.0 strap
- Protocol reference: noop's `docs/PROTOCOL_WHOOP4.md` and `docs/PROTOCOL_SENSORS.md` (repo `ryanbr/noop`).
- The phone's Bluetooth must be off: the strap serves one central at a time.
- Bond the strap the way it asks, not with `bluetoothctl pair`. A manual pair leaves command writes unanswered, and BlueZ then rejects every later write with `InProgress`. Instead: `bluetoothctl remove <mac>`, then in `bluetoothctl` run `agent NoInputNoOutput`, `default-agent`, `connect <mac>`, subscribe to `61080003`/`4`/`5` with `gatt.notify on`, and answer `yes` to the strap's "Accept pairing" request.
- `just dev calibrate` serves the calibrator on http://127.0.0.1:7878 and appends labelled takes to `$XDG_DATA_HOME/noop-gestures/takes.jsonl`; it only answers loopback Host headers and same-origin writes.
- `just dev probe [seconds] [--imu-stream] [--queries]` runs the live IMU probe: command 63 enables type-43 frames, 1928-byte ones carry 100 accel + 100 gyro samples, delivered about once per second. It prints the ATT MTU, each batch's fragment count and first-to-last fragment time, and every other frame as hex. `--queries` also sends the read-only gets 107, 130 and 65 and walks the feature-flag names with 117/118.
- A command reply is `[36, seq, command, request seq, result]` then the body; result 1 is success and 2 is pending.
- Lying unworn on 2026-11-26, the strap answered command 63 with pending and sent no type-43 frames in three probe runs. Stream timing and the double-tap event (type 48, event 14, printed by the probe as `event=14`) are unmeasured until a probe runs with it worn.
- BlueZ negotiates an ATT MTU of 247, so a batch is 8 notifications. btleplug cannot change the connection interval or PHY on Linux, and BlueZ only takes them from root: `btmgmt set-sysconfig` types 0017/0018 or `[LE]` `MinConnectionInterval`/`MaxConnectionInterval` in `main.conf`, and `btmgmt phy`. `btmgmt read-sysconfig` works unprivileged and shows the host asking for 30 to 50 ms.
- No get offers a smaller batch or a faster stream on this firmware: 107 (IMU stream) and 130 (R12 realtime stream) both read 0, 65 (packet config) reads `02 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 01 01 01 00 00 00`, and the only feature flag is `general_ab_test`.
- The u32 at frame offset 15 of type-43 frames is not Unix time on firmware seen so far; use arrival time.

## Recognizer and daemon
- Only one process may hold the strap: stop the calibrator before `just dev run`, or the daemon connects but receives no IMU data.
- `just dev evaluate` holds out one take at a time and prints the confusion matrix; run it after recording takes, before trusting `run`.
- The recognizer is 1-nearest-neighbour over 1.2 s windows (gyro/200 dps and raw accel) centred on activity peaks, where activity is gyro magnitude plus 50 dps per g that the acceleration magnitude strays from 1 g, so a knock with no rotation still registers; idle takes become negative templates at every peak above 60. Re-sweep that weight with `evaluate` after recording knock takes. A distance-margin reject rule and DTW were tried offline and lost accuracy.
- `just dev run` runs one command per gesture, listed in `command` in `src/daemon.rs`: twist scrolls the focused app down 5 notches with `ydotool`; reverse twist calls `niri msg action toggle-overview`; left and right call `focus-column-left` and `focus-column-right`, up and down call `focus-workspace-up` and `focus-workspace-down`; knock runs `openhome ir edifier mute`. Swing labels are named after their action, so the takes were relabelled when bindings moved (backups sit next to `takes.jsonl`). `ydotool` talks to the `ydotoold` user service, whose socket is `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/.ydotool_socket` rather than the client's `/tmp` default, so the daemon sets `YDOTOOL_SOCKET` when it is unset; without it `ydotool` exits 0 and does nothing. A 100-sample cooldown, about 1 s, stops a return swing from firing the opposite gesture. Latency is 0.6 to 1.6 s: the detector waits 60 samples past the peak and the strap sends one batch per second.
- The strap samples at about 104 Hz, not 100, so a batch spans about 960 ms. `Timeline` counts samples, fits the sample period to the batch arrivals and stamps along the earliest-arrival line: a late batch does not move its samples, and no sample is dropped. A stamp trails the true sample time by the fastest transport time, which is unmeasured. Arrivals are read from a monotonic clock anchored to Unix time at each connect.
- The detector is keyed on sample count and ignores timestamps except to report the peak.
- `just dev run` logs per fire how long after the peak its batch arrived, the peak's index in its batch, how many later batches the look-ahead waited for and the detector's time, then each action's spawn-to-exit time.
- `just dev benchmark` replays every take, held out, at 28 placements through `Timeline` and `Detector` as 100-sample batches. It prints recognised, wrong and missed counts, latency from the peak to the firing batch, how far the logged latency strays from it, and false fires over the long idle takes. Its 9.6 ms sample period and 60 to 120 ms transport delay are assumptions.
- 1932-byte type-43 frames carry the optical waveform: 100 signed 24-bit little-endian samples per frame from frame offset 42 with a stride of 4, so the same 100 Hz as the IMU. The bytes past sample 100 are not waveform. The channel's wavelength is unknown. Takes store it as an optional `optical` field, and the recognizer does not use it yet.
- Finger gestures were tried and dropped. A fist produces almost no wrist motion (peak activity mostly under 60), and its downward optical drift showed only about half the time. A thumb-to-index pinch, interleaved with idle takes, had an optical signal that matched idle; the first all-pinch run had separated only because of something in that session.
- `just dev record-idle --minutes 8` records one continuous idle take of normal work. `evaluate` replays every idle take of 60 s or more through the live detector, trained without that take, and prints how many gestures would have fired; that count is the false-trigger rate to drive down.
